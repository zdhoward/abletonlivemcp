# Hip Hop songwriting, melody, rhythm, and production: raw evidence (collector track)

Collected 2026-11-10. Sourced from musicological studies, hip hop production archives, Sound On Sound interviews (Metro Boomin, Mike Dean, MixedByAli, DJ Premier, J Dilla analyses), and seminal record breakdowns (Kendrick Lamar, Drake, Travis Scott, 2Pac, The Notorious B.I.G., Metro Boomin, Pop Smoke, Wu-Tang Clan).
Evidence labels:
- **FACT** = Documented for a specific recording, artist session, or verified score analysis.
- **CONVENTION** = Universally accepted genre standard across hip hop sub-genres (Boom-Bap, Trap, Drill, Melodic Rap).
- **OPINION** = Individual producer philosophy, songwriting advice, or critical essay.

## 1. Melodic Architecture in Hip Hop

### 1a. Scale Choices, Darkness, and Harmonic Moods
- **The Natural Minor (Aeolian) & Dorian Foundations**: The dominant harmonic environment for boom-bap, golden age, and soulful hip hop (J Dilla, A Tribe Called Quest, Kendrick Lamar, J. Cole). Relies heavily on minor 7th, 9th, and 11th chord voicings sampled from 1970s jazz, soul, and fusion records.
- **The Phrygian & Phrygian Dominant Modes (1, b2, b3, 4, 5, b6, b7)**: The signature sonic identity of modern Southern trap (Metro Boomin, Southside, Lex Luger, 21 Savage). The minor 2nd (semitone above the tonic) produces immediate danger, tension, paranoia, and menace.
- **The Harmonic Minor Scale (1, 2, b3, 4, 5, b6, 7)**: Heavily exploited in UK & NY Drill (Pop Smoke, Central Cee, Fivio Foreign). The raised 7th alongside a flat 6th creates an exotic augmented-second leap, giving piano and bell melodies an ominous, cinematic gothic atmosphere.
- **The Minor Pentatonic "Earworm" Hook**: In melodic rap (Juice WRLD, Lil Baby, Post Malone, Roddy Ricch), lead vocal hooks stick strictly to 3 to 5 notes in the minor pentatonic scale, facilitating instant listener memorization and sing-along capacity.

### 1b. Flow, Cadence as Melody & Pitch Inflection
- **Rhythmic Cadence as Melody**: In hip hop, flow *is* melody. Even non-melodic rap uses micro-tonal pitch variations tied to rhythmic accents. A rapper's voice functions as an auxiliary percussion instrument that interacts with the kick and snare.
- **The Triplet / "Migos Flow" (FACT)**: A metric cadence dividing beats into 3 equal 8th-note triplets over a 4/4 meter (da-da-da, da-da-da, da-da-da). Popularized by Three 6 Mafia in the 1990s and mainstreamed globally by Migos ("Versace", "Bad and Boujee"), Kendrick Lamar, and Drake.
- **Auto-Tune as Vocal Timbre & Pitch Instrument (CONVENTION)**: Modern melodic trap utilizes Auto-Tune (Antares Auto-Tune set to hard 0-5 ms retune speed) not merely to fix pitch, but to force glissando vocal lines into stepped, quantized synthesizer-like arpeggios (Travis Scott, Future, Young Thug).

## 2. Rhythmic Architectures & Groove

### 2a. Tempo Archetypes & Meter
- **Boom-Bap & Golden Age**: 84 - 96 BPM. Straight 4/4 meter. Kick on 1 and syncopated variations; sharp acoustic snare or layered rim-clap strictly on beats 2 and 4.
- **Modern Trap (The Half-Time Grid)**: 130 - 160 BPM written in the DAW, but perceived at **half-time (65 - 80 BPM)**! The kick provides syncopated downbeat movement, while the primary snare or clap hits strictly on **Beat 3** (the half-time backbeat).
- **UK / NY Drill**: 140 - 146 BPM. Distinctive sliding syncopation where the snare lands on **Beat 3 of bar 1**, followed by an unexpected syncopated hit on the **"and" of beat 4** or delayed 8th-note accents in bar 2, creating a lurching, bouncy sway.
- **West Coast G-Funk & Bay Area Hyphy**: 94 - 104 BPM. Heavy swinging 16th-note bounce, hand claps on 2 and 4, and rolling live bass or synth basslines.

### 2b. Groove, Swing & Humanization
- **The MPC Swing Factor (54% - 62% 16th Swing)**: Pioneered by Roger Linn on the Akai MPC60/3000 and perfected by DJ Premier and Pete Rock. Shifting every second 16th note slightly late creates the head-nodding groove of classic hip hop.
- **J Dilla's "Drunk / Unquantized" Time (FACT)**: J Dilla deliberately disabled quantization on the MPC3000, playing kicks slightly ahead of the beat while delaying snares or dragging hi-hats. This creates polyrhythmic micro-timing tension between elements, now emulated globally across modern lo-fi and neo-soul hip hop.
- **Trap Hi-Hat Rolls & Pitch Automation**: Unlike static acoustic hi-hats, trap hi-hats use repeating 1/16th or 1/8th patterns interrupted by sudden subdivisions: 1/32 notes, 1/32 triplets (1/48), and 1/64 rolls, automated with downward pitch glides and panning sweeps.

## 3. Songwriting & Harmonic Progressions

### 3a. Harmonic Loops and Sample Chops
- **The 2-Bar to 4-Bar Repeating Loop (CONVENTION)**: Unlike rock or classical music with functional chord progressions resolving to the tonic (V7 -> I), hip hop harmony is circular. Chord loops loop indefinitely without traditional resolution, establishing an unshakeable hypnotic groove over which vocal cadences evolve.
- **Common Progressions**:
  - Sad / Introspective Trap: i - VI - III - VII (e.g. C#m - A - E - B; Juice WRLD "Lucid Dreams", Post Malone).
  - Dark / Menacing Trap: i - bII (Phrygian vamp: e.g. Am - Bb or single-note root drone; Metro Boomin, 21 Savage).
  - Soulful Boom-Bap: i9 - iv7 - bVII9 - IIImaj7 (jazz turnaround loop; J Dilla, Nas).
- **Sample Manipulation (The Modern Sound)**: Frank Dukes, Cubeatz, and Murda Beatz create original instrumental melodies (piano, flute, acoustic guitar, bells), render them to audio, pitch-shift them down 2-5 semitones, slow them down by 50% (half-time via tape or plugin), and apply low-pass filtering and vinyl wow/flutter.

### 3b. Song Structure in Modern Hip Hop
- **The "Hook-First" Blueprint**: Modern streaming-era trap frequently skips long instrumental intros:
  - Intro (4-8 bars: filtered melody only) -> Hook (8-16 bars: full beat drop) -> Verse 1 (16 bars) -> Hook (8-16 bars) -> Verse 2 (12-16 bars) -> Hook (8-16 bars) -> Outro (4-8 bars: fade or beat breakdown).
- **Song Length**: Concentrated between 2:15 and 3:00 minutes; bridges and guitar solos are almost entirely omitted to maximize streaming replay value.

## 4. Production, Arrangement & Sound Design

### 4a. Arrangement Contrast & Beat Drops
- **The Filtered Intro**: Starting with the core melodic sample run through a low-pass filter (LPF at 500-800 Hz) to create a muffled "underwater" sound; opening the filter completely on the beat drop downbeat.
- **The 808/Kick Dropout (Tension Generation)**: 1 or 2 bars prior to the hook or during critical punchlines, the producer completely cuts out the 808 bass, kick, and hi-hats, leaving only the rapper's vocal and a sparse melodic element. When the full 808 slides back in on the downbeat, the dynamic contrast hits with maximum physical weight.
- **808 Glides / Pitch Slides (Drill & Trap)**: Writing rapid pitch glides (portamento) on the 808 MIDI track, sliding up an octave (+12 semitones) or down 5 semitones to create melody within the sub-bass foundation.

### 4b. Instrumentation Hierarchy
1. **Low End**: Roland TR-808 sub-bass (distorted fundamental) + Acoustic/digital Kick drum transient.
2. **Backbeat**: Snare / Clap combination (crisp high-mid transient on beat 3).
3. **Timekeeping**: Trap closed hi-hats (panned slightly off-center) + open hi-hat on the upbeat ("and" of 2 and 4).
4. **Melodic Core**: Dark grand piano, plucked music box / bell, acoustic guitar loop, or detuned synth flute.
5. **Atmosphere / FX**: Reverse crashes, gunshots/foley, vocal chants ("Hey!", "Yeah!"), riser sweeps, and producer audio tags (e.g., "Metro Boomin want some more...").
