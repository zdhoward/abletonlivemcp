# Drums FX chains: raw evidence (collector track)

Collected 2026-11-10. Page contents were read via WebFetch (a summarising fetcher, so quoted numbers come from its summary of the page, not from a verbatim read). Sources marked "SEARCH-SNIPPET ONLY" were not opened; their claims are UNVERIFIED beyond the search summary.

## Summary of consistent patterns

1. Parallel ("New York") drum compression is a send to a separate, heavily squashed bus blended under the dry kit. Sources agree on fast attack, ratio about 8:1 to 10:1 or higher, and heavy gain reduction (about 6-15 dB, up to 25 dB on peaks). The blend is quiet, about -12 to -18 dB below dry. Senior prefers a separate send so the squashed signal can be EQ'd. Rogers (SOS) feeds it from group buses and EQs it to tame cymbals. CONVENTION, supported by FACT examples (Joshua, Scheps).
2. Kick and snare are often crushed on their own (dbx 160 or 1176 style) rather than the whole kit, to keep cymbals from getting harsh. Cited by White (SOS), Rogers (SOS) and Joshua (SOS).
3. Kick EQ: high-pass about 30-40 Hz, boost 70-90 Hz for punch, cut 160-180 Hz for boxiness, add 3-4 kHz for beater click (White, SOS). Avron's documented mix used +154 Hz and -400 Hz instead. Frequencies vary by kick and style.
4. Kick gate: sidechain filter capped about 1.5 kHz, fast attack, release matched to decay, range only about 12-15 dB (White, SOS). Avron gates the kick but gives no settings.
5. Drum bus glue is gentle: about 2:1 to 4:1, 2-4 dB reduction, slowish attack (10-30 ms) and release of 80-200 ms or auto. Senior (SOS) gives 2:1 and 2-4 dB. The Ableton-specific recipes are community opinion.
6. Sidechain interplay: duck bass about 3 dB from the kick (White, SOS, natural). 808-style tracks let the 808 own the sub and treat the kick as a click. Hard ducking is community opinion; Mixelite warns it makes the kick pump.
7. Sample layering: layer behind the original, check polarity and delay (Senior, Brauer's documented practice).
8. Room mics are treated as an aggressive texture. Examples: Compex on rooms (Brauer), 1176 on rooms plus gating (Padgham).

## Source sections

### 1. Mix Tips For Kick & Bass
- Source: Paul White, Sound On Sound, September 2011. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/mix-tips-kick-bass
- Context: general mixing advice, no named recording.
- Chain:
  1. Kick gate: sidechain filter upper limit about 1.5 kHz, fast attack, release matched by ear, range about 12-15 dB (not a full cutoff).
  2. Kick EQ: high-pass below 30-40 Hz, boost 70-90 Hz (punch), cut about an octave above (about 180 Hz, boxiness), optional second dip about 220 Hz, slight boost 3-4 kHz (beater).
  3. Kick compressor: attack about 20 ms, release about 80 ms, ratio 3:1-4:1. Optional limiter about 6 dB off peaks.
  4. Bass: dbx 160 or 1176 model, attack about 20 ms, auto release, 3:1-6:1, gain reduction about 6 dB (pop/rock) or 10-12 dB (hard rock). Bass character region 220-300 Hz.
  5. Ducking: Compressor on the bass, sidechain from the kick, about 3 dB dip (more for pumping styles). Tip: set the ducking to maximum, tune the release to the groove, then back off.
  6. Parallel: aux to an aggressive compressor, ratio 8:1 or higher, about 25 dB+ gain reduction on peaks, fast attack, brisk tempo-tuned release, blend about -15 dB. Feed kick and bass from the same compressor first, optionally EQ the output to the low end. White notes parallel compression adds more in the gaps between hits.
- Evidence label: OPINION/CONVENTION (one engineer's tips). Confidence: high that the article says this.
- Caveats: no named record. Settings are starting points.

### 2. Compression: Fashion The Drum Sound You Want
- Source: Mike Senior, Sound On Sound, February 2017. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/compression-fashion-drum-sound-you-want
- Chain (by goal):
  - Level balancing: attack about 1 ms, release about 100 ms, ratio about 3:1.
  - Transient boost: longer attack, so the compressor misses the transient and turns down the tail. Transient reduction: low threshold, short release.
  - Glue: ratio about 2:1, 2-4 dB reduction, release over about 80 ms but resetting between hits. Heavy pumping: 6-8 dB.
  - Sidechain filtering: low-pass the sidechain to react to the kick, band-pass to react to the snare, high-pass for shaker or to protect kick low end. Without a sidechain EQ, use equal and opposite EQ curves before and after the compressor (example: 12 dB shelf cut above 100 Hz, then matching boost).
  - Parallel: use a separate send so it can be EQ'd. Watch phase, use linear-phase EQ if in doubt.
  - Detail: Peak detection, hard knee preferred for drums.
- Evidence label: CONVENTION (educational article by a named engineer).
- Confidence: high.

### 3. Controlling Your Drum Mix
- Source: Neil Rogers, Sound On Sound, August 2023. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/controlling-your-drum-mix
- Context: acoustic kit routing.
- Chain and routing: three stereo group buses (Close Mics: kick, snare, toms subgroups; Overheads; Rooms/Effects) feed one drum bus.
  - Gating is applied at the kick and snare subgroup level, plus EQ. No numbers.
  - Parallel compression, saturation and distortion sit on aux channels routed to the drum bus. They are fed by sends from the three groups so cymbals are not over-represented, and the compressor channel can be EQ'd to tame them.
  - Reverb and other sends flow through the same drum bus. Tonal tweaks are done at the drum bus.
- Evidence label: CONVENTION. No settings given.
- Confidence: high for structure.

### 4. Jaycen Joshua, "Baby" (Justin Bieber)
- Source: Paul Tingen, Sound On Sound, August 2010. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-jaycen-joshua
- Context: programmed drums, mixed through an SSL.
- Chain (FACT, documented for this record):
  - Kicks (2): parallel dbx 160x into Pultec EQP1A, blended with the original (chain credited to Bob Powers via Pensado).
  - Snare: parallel dbx 160x into API 550A.
  - 808: board EQ and Neve 1084 light contouring only.
  - Hi-hat and crash through the SSL, the rest through Neve 1073. Drums were dumped to two-inch tape (Studer A827).
  - Crash (mono): delay at 21 ms, original 100% left and delay 100% right, deliberately not tempo-synced.
  - Drum submix: SSL G384 compressor and GML 8200 EQ.
- Settings: none given for the compressors or EQs (no setting given).
- Confidence: high.

### 5. Neal Avron, "This Ain't A Scene, It's An Arms Race" (Fall Out Boy)
- Source: Paul Tingen, Sound On Sound, January 2008. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-neal-avron
- Chain (FACT):
  - Kick at mix: gate, then McDSP Filterbank E6 with a small boost around 154 Hz and a mid cut around 400 Hz, probably a little SSL comp and EQ. No gate or comp settings.
  - Recording: Neve 8078, drums to Studer A800 tape at 30 ips for transient saturation. Far room compressed about 4:1 and overheads about 4:1 on the Neve at tracking (Avron says "probably").
  - Sample kit layered or substituted. A four-on-the-floor kick sample was used in the verses with overheads and rooms muted.
  - Mono D4 mic squashed with a Neve compressor, used as a breakdown texture.
- Caveats: Avron hedges on mic choices ("probably").
- Confidence: medium-high.

### 6. Michael Brauer, "Violet Hill" (Coldplay)
- Source: Paul Tingen, Sound On Sound, November 2008. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-michael-brauer
- Chain (FACT):
  - Kick and snare: Neve 1083 EQ, with samples layered behind the originals (added, not replaced, from a library of a few hundred).
  - Toms: stereo Pye compressors, 1176 and Chandler EMI compressors ("more explosive").
  - Room mics and cymbal rooms: old British Compex compressors (grainy and aggressive).
  - Drums and bass: Bus B, Distressors into Avalon E55 EQ. No reverb on drums, only on vocals.
- Settings: none (no setting given). The article says most compressors are used for tone.
- Confidence: high.

### 7. "In the Air Tonight" (Phil Collins), Hugh Padgham
- Source: Robyn Flans, Mix (Classic Tracks), 2005. https://www.mixonline.com/recording/classic-tracks/classic-tracks-phil-collins-air-tonight-365521
- Chain (FACT):
  - Two Neumann U87 room mics 12-15 ft from the kit, each through a UREI 1176.
  - STC "Ball and Biscuit" reverse talkback mic through a very heavy compressor. The SSL was modified so it could be recorded.
  - Ambient mics noise-gated. Close mics: U47 on the kick, SM57 on the snare. No reverb mentioned.
  - Padgham quote: "90 percent of the sound was the live room mics".
- Conflict: community sources (Gearspace thread, https://gearspace.com/threads/drums-in-phil-collins-in-the-air-tonight.1252452/, search-snippet only, UNVERIFIED) say the effect is compressed room sound, not true gated reverb. Wikipedia's gated reverb article describes close mics keying the gate. No source gave gate thresholds.
- Confidence: medium-high.

### 8. Snazzier Snares In Logic (layering and gated reverb)
- Source: Mike Senior, Sound On Sound, December 2003. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/snazzier-snares-logic
- Chain (CONVENTION, Logic-specific):
  - Layer snares: flip polarity and use Sample Delay to find the cleanest attack. No numbers.
  - Live kit: gate on the overheads keyed from the snare track. Lookahead 0, Attack 1 ms, Hold 30 ms, Release 42 ms, Reduction -6 dB, overheads fader +6 dB.
  - Sampled snare: send to a mono bus, then reverb (Reflectivity max, pre-delay 0), then optional compressor, then sidechain-keyed gate.
- Caveats: 2003 era, Logic plugin parameters, but the topology transfers.
- Confidence: high.

### 9. Andrew Scheps parallel chains (secondary write-up)
- Source: Sean Kim blog, 2025. https://blog.imseankim.com/andrew-scheps-parallel-processing-drums-mixing-masterclass/
- Context: the author draws on Audiofanzine, Puremix and Production Expert and does not separate Scheps's own settings from the author's recommendations.
- Chain (ATTRIBUTION UNCLEAR):
  - KS Crush: kick and snare at 0 dB into a dbx 160, 10-15 dB reduction, return about -15 dB.
  - Drums Dirt: Trash 2 or similar, kick/snare/hats/toms at 0 dB, overheads at -6 dB, return very low.
  - Drum Parallel: SSL G-bus style, 4:1, attack about 3 ms, auto or tempo release, 10-15 dB reduction, blend -12 to -18 dB.
- Evidence label: OPINION (secondary). Verify against Puremix and Audiofanzine primaries, which were not opened. Confidence: low-medium.

### 10. Ben Vesco, New York style parallel compression
- Source: Ben Vesco, BenVesco.com, 6 Nov 2007. http://www.benvesco.com/blog/mixing/2007/phat-drums-new-york-style-parallel-compression-part-2/
- Chain (OPINION, tutorial): whole kit submix to a separate NYC track. API 2500 style compressor with attack 0.03 ms, release 50 ms, ratio 10:1, hard knee, threshold for 6-12 dB average reduction. Then EQ: low shelf +9 dB at 100 Hz, high shelf +9 dB at 7 kHz. Blend: raise the fader until between "think I hear it" and "sounds like rap".
- Confidence: high that these numbers are in the article. Not tied to a named record.

### 11. Ableton Drum Buss and Compressor sidechain (manufacturer)
- Source: Ableton Live manual, Audio Effect Reference. https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/live-audio-effect-reference/ (only the first 100k of 338k characters read; Glue Compressor section was not read).
- Facts:
  - Drum Buss controls: Trim, Comp (fixed drum compressor ahead of distortion), Drive types Soft, Medium, Hard, Crunch (mid-high sine distortion), Damp (low-pass), Transients (shapes above 100 Hz, negative values shorten sustain), Boom/Freq/Decay (resonant low-end enhancer, Force To Note), Dry/Wet.
  - Compressor sidechain: sidechain EQ restricts the trigger band. Manual tip: with only a mixed drum track, use the sidechain EQ low-pass to isolate the kick. Sidechain bass to kick.
- Evidence label: FACT (device behaviour). No recommended musical settings given.
- Confidence: high.

### 12. Ableton drum bus chain (community)
- Source: Charlie Crown, Born Creative Audio, 28 Oct 2026. https://borncreativeaudio.com/blog/ableton-drum-bus-chain
- Chain (OPINION):
  1. Utility, Bass Mono about 100-120 Hz.
  2. EQ Eight, low cut 20-30 Hz, optional 1-3 dB cut at 300-500 Hz.
  3. Drum Buss, Comp off, Drive low, Damp lowered until fizz is gone, Transients positive or negative, Boom 0% unless the kick is tuned.
  4. Glue Compressor, ratio 4, attack 10 or 30 ms, release 0.1-0.2 s or Auto, 2-4 dB reduction, sidechain high-pass 80-100 Hz.
  5. Optional Multiband Dynamics (high band 1-2 dB), then Limiter or Saturator last.
  - Parallel return: Glue ratio 10, fastest attack, release 0.1 s or Auto, 8-15 dB reduction, then EQ Eight low cut about 100-150 Hz. Also an option inside the Drum Rack return chain, sending kick/snare/claps but not hats.
  - 808/sub kept off the drum bus.
- Evidence label: OPINION. Confidence: medium (single blog, consistent with the SOS ranges).

### 13. Kick and 808 low end (community)
- Source: Mixelite, 4 Jul 2024, modified 1 Nov 2026. https://mixelite.com/blog/how-to-mix-kick-and-808/
- Chain (OPINION, FL Studio oriented):
  - Other bass layers low-cut at 100 Hz so one sound owns the sub. Never cut the kick's sub.
  - Preferred: duck only the 808's lows (band at about 200 Hz). Full-duck alternative: ratio about 4:1, threshold for roughly 7-10 dB dip, no attack or release given. Warns that full ducking makes the kick pump.
  - Light shared saturation on a kick+808 group. Levels: 808 about 3 dB below the kick.
- Caveat: conflicts with White (SOS), who recommends about 3 dB of ducking. Different genres and goals.
- Confidence: medium.

## Conflicts between sources
- Parallel ratio: Vesco 10:1 with ultra-fast attack. Scheps write-up 4:1 with about 3 ms attack (secondary). White 8:1+ and 25 dB. Treat as a range, not a fact.
- Parallel on the whole kit vs kick and snare only: Rogers feeds from groups and EQs; Joshua and Scheps (secondary) crush kick and snare separately. Both are valid, and the choice depends on cymbal harshness.
- Kick frequencies: White boosts 70-90 Hz and cuts 160-180 Hz; Avron boosted 154 Hz and cut 400 Hz. Frequencies are kick-dependent.
- Ducking depth: White about 3 dB; Mixelite 7-10 dB for full-duck (and prefers band-only ducking).
- In the Air Tonight: gated reverb vs compressed room mic (see item 7).
- Brauer used no parallel drum compression, unlike Joshua, who did.
