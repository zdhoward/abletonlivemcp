# Drum Patterns, Playing Techniques, and Programming: raw evidence (collector track)

Collected 2026-11-10. Sourced from drum machine programming treatises (260 Drum Machine Patterns by Rene-Pierre Bardet), funk and hip hop drumming literature, and electronic music performance masterclasses.
Evidence labels:
- **FACT** = Documented for a specific genre standard, rhythm analysis, or artist session.
- **CONVENTION** = Universally accepted groove and pattern convention.
- **OPINION** = Individual producer philosophy or personal performance preference.

---

## 1. Genre Pattern Archetypes

### 1a. Four-on-the-Floor (House, Disco, Techno, Trance) (CONVENTION / FACT)
- **Kick**: Solid quarter notes on beats 1, 2, 3, 4.
- **Snare / Clap**: Layered on beats 2 and 4.
- **Hi-Hat**: Closed hat on downbeats (1, 2, 3, 4), wide **Open Hi-Hat on the upbeat ("and")** of every beat: (1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &).
- **Ghost Percussion**: Congas, shakers, and rim clicks placed on the 2nd and 4th sixteenth notes (the "e" and "a" of 1-e-&-a).

### 1b. Trap and Drill (CONVENTION / FACT)
- **Tempo & Grid**: 130 - 160 BPM written in DAW (half-time feel: 65 - 80 BPM).
- **Kick**: Syncopated downbeat movement (Beat 1, followed by syncopated 16th-note hits often landing on the "and" of beat 2 or the "a" of beat 3).
- **Clap / Snare**: Locked strictly on **Beat 3** (the half-time backbeat).
- **Hi-Hats**: High-speed switches between 8ths, 16ths, 32nds, and 1/48 (triplet) rolls with pitch drops and velocity ramps.
- **Drill Variation**: Snare lands on Beat 3 in bar 1, then syncopates to the "and" of beat 4 in bar 2.

### 1c. Boom-Bap & Golden Age Hip Hop (CONVENTION / FACT)
- **Tempo**: 85 - 95 BPM. Straight 4/4 meter.
- **Kick**: Heavy downbeat on 1; syncopated upbeat kick on the "and" of beat 2 or "and" of beat 3.
- **Snare**: Punchy acoustic snare or layered rim-clap strictly on beats 2 and 4.
- **Hi-Hat**: Steady 8th or 16th notes with Roger Linn 54% - 62% swing. Accenting downbeat hits (velocity 100) and softening upbeat hits (velocity 70).

### 1d. Breakbeats, Jungle, and Drum & Bass (FACT)
- **The Amen Break (The Winstons – "Amen, Brother", 1969)**: 4-bar drum solo by Gregory Coleman. Sliced, pitched up 4 to 8 semitones, and resequenced at 165 - 175 BPM.
- **Two-Step D&B Pattern**:
  - Kick on Beat 1.
  - Snare on Beat 2.
  - Syncopated Kick on the "and" of Beat 3.
  - Snare on Beat 4.
  - Ghost snares dancing on 16th offbeats at velocity 40-60.

### 1e. Afrobeat, Reggaeton, and Dembow (FACT)
- **The Dembow Rhythm (3:3:2 Polyrhythm)**:
  - Kick: Four-on-the-floor quarter notes (1, 2, 3, 4).
  - Snare / Rimshot: Hits on the dotted 8th note syncopation: Beat 1, the "and" of 2, and the "a" of 3. (1 . . & . 3 . a 4).
  - Creates the irresistible Caribbean/Latin sway found in Reggaeton, Dancehall, and Afrobeats.

## 2. Drum Machine Playing and Performance Techniques

### 2a. Finger Drumming on 4x4 Pads (CONVENTION / FACT)
- **Standard Pad Layout (The Ergonomic 4x4 Standard)**:
  - Row 1 (Bottom): Pad 1 = Kick, Pad 2 = Snare/Rim, Pad 3 = Closed Hi-Hat, Pad 4 = Open Hi-Hat.
  - Row 2: Pad 5 = Low Tom / Perc 1, Pad 6 = Mid Tom / Perc 2, Pad 7 = High Tom / Shaker, Pad 8 = Clap.
  - Row 3: Pad 9 = Crash 1, Pad 10 = Ride, Pad 11 = Ride Bell, Pad 12 = Crash 2.
  - Row 4 (Top): Pad 13-16 = Samples, Vocal Chops, Fills.
- **Two-Handed Hi-Hat Technique**: Using index and middle fingers of the right hand alternating (R-L-R-L) for fast, smooth 16th-note hat patterns, while the left hand operates kick (thumb) and snare (index finger).

### 2b. Step Sequencing and Accent Dynamics
- **The TR-808/909 Step Sequencing Workflow**: 16 physical step keys representing 16th notes of a 4/4 bar.
- **The Accent Lane**: Programmable accent step that applies global gain and filter envelope brightness to all voices triggered on that step.
- **Note Repeat / Roll Button**: Pressing a pad while holding Note Repeat (1/16, 1/32, or triplet) uses pad pressure (aftertouch) to swell velocity dynamically.

### 2c. Micro-Timing, Swing, and Humanization
- **MPC Swing Ranges**:
  - 50% = Perfectly straight (no swing).
  - 54% - 58% = Subtle head-nod groove (classic boom-bap, UK garage, tech-house).
  - 60% - 66% = Heavy shuffle/bounce (swing hip hop, neo-soul).
- **The Dilla "Drunk" Timing (FACT)**: Deliberate manual de-quantization. Moving kicks slightly ahead of the beat (rushing) and pulling snares or hi-hats slightly behind the beat (dragging), creating polyrhythmic micro-tension.
