# Rock songwriting, melody, rhythm, and production: raw evidence (collector track)

Collected 2026-11-10. Sourced from musicological analyses, Berklee College of Music songwriting curricula, Sound On Sound producer retrospectives (Andy Wallace, Butch Vig, Rick Rubin, Brendan O'Brien), and seminal rock record analyses (Led Zeppelin, The Beatles, Pink Floyd, Nirvana, Foo Fighters, Queens of the Stone Age, Radiohead, Metallica).
Evidence labels:
- **FACT** = Documented for a specific recording, artist session, or verified score analysis.
- **CONVENTION** = Universally accepted genre standard across multiple rock eras (Classic, Grunge, Alternative, Metal).
- **OPINION** = Individual producer philosophy, songwriting advice, or critical essay.

## 1. Melodic Architecture in Rock

### 1a. Scale Choices and Modal Color
- **Minor Pentatonic & The Blues Scale (1, b3, 4, b5, 5, b7)**: The bedrock of classic and hard rock (Led Zeppelin, AC/DC, Black Sabbath). The diminished 5th ("blue note") provides signature grit, tension, and dissonance against major/power chord accompaniment.
- **The Mixolydian Mode (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, b7)**: Essential for classic, Southern, and anthem rock (The Rolling Stones, Guns N' Roses "Sweet Child O' Mine", AC/DC). The flat-7th avoids the polite classical leading tone (natural 7) while maintaining a bright, driving major 3rd.
- **The Dorian Mode (1, 2, b3, 4, 5, 6, b7)**: The bridge between minor darkness and major lift (Pink Floyd, Santana, Soundgarden). The natural 6th prevents the gloomy sadness of Aeolian natural minor, injecting swagger and blues-rock funk.
- **The Aeolian Mode (Natural Minor: 1, 2, b3, 4, 5, b6, b7)**: The staple of 90s grunge, alternative, and modern hard rock (Nirvana, Alice in Chains, Linkin Park). The minor 6th provides melancholy and dramatic emotional gravity.

### 1b. Melodic Phrasing, Riffs, and Vocal Delivery
- **Riff-As-Hook & Riff-Vocal Counterpoint (CONVENTION)**: In rock, the guitar riff frequently acts as the primary melodic hook rather than just rhythm accompaniment (e.g., Deep Purple "Smoke On The Water", Nirvana "Smells Like Teen Spirit", The White Stripes "Seven Nation Army").
- **Call-and-Response Phrasing (CONVENTION)**: Originating in gospel and delta blues, rock arrangement consistently alternates between vocal statements and instrumental fills. Led Zeppelin ("Whole Lotta Love", "Black Dog") and Queen ("Bohemian Rhapsody") structure phrases where the singer pauses for 2-4 beats while a guitar/drum fill replies.
- **Tessitura and Vocal Intensity Shifts (FACT)**: Verse lines reside in comfortable conversational chest voice or baritone register (e.g. Kurt Cobain, Chris Cornell, Dave Grohl); choruses leap an octave or major 6th into full-throttle chest/mixed belt or grit/screaming, triggering emotional catharsis through raw acoustic energy.

## 2. Rhythmic Architectures & Groove

### 2a. Tempos and Feel
- **Tempo Archetypes**:
  - Classic Mid-Tempo Groove: 115 - 130 BPM (AC/DC "Back in Black" @ 90-92 half-time / 184; Foo Fighters "Everlong" @ 158 driving; Led Zeppelin "Rock and Roll" @ 170).
  - Heavy Grunge / Stoner Rock: 75 - 95 BPM (Nirvana "Come As You Are" @ 120; Soundgarden "Outshined" @ 102; Queens of the Stone Age "No One Knows" @ 171 swing).
  - Punk / High-Energy Rock: 155 - 195 BPM (Green Day "Basket Case" @ 175; Blink-182 "What's My Age Again" @ 158).
  - Rock Ballad / Slow Burn: 65 - 80 BPM (Pink Floyd "Comfortably Numb" @ 64; Aerosmith "Dream On" @ 80).
- **Meter & Subdivisions**: Primarily 4/4 driving straight 8th notes on guitars/bass. Blues-rock and classic metal frequently employ 6/8 or 12/8 shuffle/triplet swing (Led Zeppelin "Dazed and Confused", Jimi Hendrix "Red House"). Progressive rock exploits odd meters (7/4 in Pink Floyd "Money", 7/8 in Soundgarden "Them Bones", 5/4 in Tool "Vicarious").

### 2b. Pocket, Timing & Humanization (Grid vs. Push/Pull)
- **The "Behind-the-Beat" Drag (Bonham Pocket)**: John Bonham (Led Zeppelin) and Chad Smith (Red Hot Chili Peppers) lock the bass drum strictly on the downbeat while laying the snare micro-seconds *behind* the beat, generating massive perceived size and heavy groove.
- **The "Ahead-of-the-Beat" Rush (Punk & Urgency)**: Dave Grohl (Nirvana) and Tre Cool (Green Day) lean micro-seconds *ahead* of the click on upbeats and snare hits, imparting adrenaline, urgency, and aggressive forward momentum.
- **Hi-Hat & Ride Dynamics (Sectional Delineation)**:
  - Verses: Tight, closed hi-hats played softly or muted guitar arpeggios.
  - Pre-chorus: Open hi-hat sloshes or building snare builds.
  - Chorus: Transitioning to the heavy Ride cymbal bell or a wide-open crash cymbal wash (riding the crash) that fills the high-frequency stereo spectrum.

## 3. Songwriting & Harmonic Progressions

### 3a. Chord Progressions and Modal Borrowing
- **The Power Chord (Root + 5th, No 3rd)**: By eliminating the 3rd, power chords avoid muddy harmonic intermodulation when running through high-gain tube distortion. This leaves melodic interpretation open for the vocal to sing minor or major intervals on top.
- **The Flat-VII (bVII) and Flat-VI (bVI) Subtonic Progressions (CONVENTION)**:
  - Classic Rock: I - bVII - IV - I (e.g., Lynyrd Skynyrd "Sweet Home Alabama", AC/DC "Highway to Hell", The Rolling Stones "Sympathy for the Devil").
  - Grunge / Alt-Rock: i - bVI - bIII - bVII (Aeolian progression: Nirvana "Smells Like Teen Spirit" in F minor; Pearl Jam "Alive").
  - Chromatic Shift Riffing: Riffs built on parallel semitone movements (Black Sabbath "Iron Man", Metallica "Enter Sandman", Alice in Chains "Man in the Box").

### 3b. Song Structure & Tension-Release Blueprints
- **The Standard Rock Form**: Intro (Riff) -> Verse 1 -> Pre-Chorus -> Chorus 1 -> Re-Intro (Half-Riff) -> Verse 2 -> Pre-Chorus -> Chorus 2 -> Guitar Solo / Bridge -> Chorus 3 (Double Chorus) -> Outro (Riff Vamp / Fade or Hard Hit).
- **The "Loud-Quiet-Loud" Dynamic (Pixies / Nirvana Blueprint) (FACT)**:
  - Verses: Stripped down to single bassline, kick/snare, and minimal clean guitar or silence.
  - Pre-Chorus: Rising snare rolls, swelling cymbals, or creeping guitar feedback creating unbearable anticipation.
  - Chorus: Explosive wall of sound; all guitars engage distortion, bass doubles guitar riff, drummer rides crash cymbals, vocalist belts at maximum volume.

## 4. Production, Arrangement & Sound Design

### 4a. Frequency Orchestration & Arrangement
- **Rhythm Guitars Quad-Tracking**: Two distinct guitar/amp/mic pairings tracked twice and hard-panned: Guitar 1 (Left 100%, Right 100% - e.g. Gibson Les Paul into Marshall JCM800); Guitar 2 (Left 80%, Right 80% - e.g. Fender Telecaster into Mesa Boogie or Vox AC30). The tonal contrast creates thickness without phase-cancellation smearing.
- **Bass & Kick Marriage**: The bass guitar locks note-for-note with the kick drum pattern. When the kick plays an 8th-note push, the bass guitar plays the identical syncopation.
- **Subtle Midrange Fillers (The Secret Weapon)**: Rick Rubin and Butch Vig frequently bury a Hammond B3 organ, mellotron, or clean acoustic guitar deep under heavy electric guitar walls at -15 to -20 dB. The listener cannot identify the organ, but muting it causes the entire guitar wall to sound thin and hollow.

### 4b. Transitions and Ear Candy
- **Pick Scrapes & String Slides**: Leading into the chorus downbeat.
- **Amp Feedback & Tube Hum**: Natural organic noise left unmuted in intro/outro breaks to give human authenticity.
- **Drum Fills as Structural Signposts**: Rather than keeping constant rhythm, rock drummers use expressive tom fills to announce section transitions.
