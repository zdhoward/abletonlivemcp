# Vocals FX chains: raw evidence (collector output, 2026-11-10)

Method: pages fetched with WebFetch (summarised by a small model, so quotes/numbers are as relayed by the fetch tool; re-check critical numbers against the page). Labels: FACT = documented by engineer/manufacturer for a named record or workflow; CONVENTION = widely repeated technique; OPINION = blog/forum view. "No setting given" means the page gave none.

## Summary of the most consistent patterns

1. Effects order: pitch correction/cleanup, then EQ and/or compression, then de-ess, then time effects on sends (iZotope; the SOS pieces list the same pattern). Order of EQ vs compressor vs de-esser is explicitly disputed (see conflicts).
2. Reverb/delay on aux sends, not inserts, with the return filtered (HP/LP) and often a pre-delay so the dry vocal stays forward (Pensado: 16th or 32nd pre-delay, Eventide 2016, top rolled off ~5k, bottom from 300 Hz; Meshplugins throw: HP ~400 Hz, LP 4-5 kHz; Jason Moss: "a little pre-delay").
3. FET (1176-style) compression is the most-named lead-vocal compressor in named records (CLA/My Chemical Romance, Ghenea/Hanes, Taesub Lee CLA-76, Riordan CLA-76, Elmhirst/Adele), often followed by an optical or leveler (LA-2A/LA-3A/CL1B) or put in parallel (Scheps, Weeknd "The Hills", Kendrick guests). Amount: iZotope says 3-7 dB GR; Ghenea/Hanes ~3 dB (Blackie 4:1) to ~10 dB (Bluey 4:1); Ogan 3-6 dB.
4. De-ess frequency is vocal-dependent: stated values 5449 and 11004 Hz (Adele), ~5500 Hz ~6 dB (Hanes), 6557 Hz (Eilish), "above 3 kHz" / 4-10 kHz range, as low as 1.5 kHz (iZotope).
5. Width/doubling comes from short pitch-shift/doubler sends (MicroShift, Doubler, Little AlterBoy, H3000 detune) rather than recorded doubles in pop (Lee/TWICE, Weeknd, Eilish "Width" aux, Pensado).
6. Delays are tempo-synced: 1/8, dotted 1/8, 1/4 (CLA, Ghenea/Hanes, Lee, Dua Lipa chop); slap ~16th dark delay (Pensado "Buttons"). Throws: send automation at -inf with a short spike, not the mix knob (single vendor source, plus community).
7. Backing vocals get a different chain/space than the lead (own bus, one shared reverb, more HP, different EQ): Lee, Weeknd, Eilish, Ogan.
8. Counter-example: dry vocals are a legitimate choice (Eilish "Bad Guy": reverb asked to be removed; Joshua: reverb "the kiss of death on rap vocals"; Elmhirst "Hello": no compression, only de-essing and rides).

## 1. Dave Pensado, Pussycat Dolls "Beep" / "Buttons" (pop)
- Source: "Secrets Of The Mix Engineers: David Pensado", Paul Tingen, Sound On Sound, Jan 2007. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-david-pensado
- Context: lead vocal, Nicole Scherzinger, pop.
- Chain "Beep" lead: (1) Waves Linear Phase EQ then McDSP Filter Bank (broad shaping then colour; he sometimes reversed order; rejected a Neve 1073 as too generic; no band settings). (2) Effects: Waves Supertap with 149/298/587 ms delays (16th/8th/half note), spread and rising in level left to right, lets the vocal sit ~0.5 dB lower; then Waves Doubler (four delays, some direct sound); then SoundToys Pitch Blender (a few cents flat/sharp, constantly changing; no values). (3) Reverb: Digidesign Revibe, modified Large Natural Plate with a dip at 8k (no decay/pre-delay given).
- "Buttons": Line 6 Echo Farm with distortion and high wow/flutter; Telray variable delay ~16th, dark, inserted on the track; Waves Metaflanger parallel, mix 21; middle-eastern section Reverb One 11.5 s, low end rolled off, mids boosted, from an aux, into Metaflanger.
- General: he dislikes compression and keeps dynamics; Trans-X Multi used on vocals to restore top end; for Eventide 2016 stereo room: 16th or 32nd pre-delay, roll off top at ~5k (sometimes 4k) and bottom from 300 Hz (stated as preference, not confirmed for this vocal).
- Label: FACT (named record). Confidence: high; caveat: second-hand fetch summary, 2007 plug-in era.

## 2. Cian Riordan, St Vincent "Pay Your Way In Pain" (art-rock/pop)
- Source: "Inside Track: St Vincent 'Pay Your Way In Pain'", Paul Tingen, SOS, Aug 2021. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-st-vincent-pay-your-way-pain
- Chain on lead aux (listed order, article does not confirm it as signal order): FabFilter Pro-MB (pulls midrange), Waves CLA-76 (overall compression), Waves API 550A (brightness), oeksound Soothe (harshness), Pro-Q 3 (highs). No numbers.
- Parallel distortion sends: SansAmp aux and Decapitator (A mode, Ampex 351 model), set aggressively; distortion exposed harshness at 3.5 kHz and 7.5 kHz, which he then removed from the source track.
- Reverb: aux send, Eventide SP2016 room. Delay: none specified for lead.
- Vocal sub bus: SoundToys Tremolator, Slate Fairchild, Vari-Mu, Pro-Q 3 dynamic EQ on ~2 kHz.
- Label: FACT. Confidence: medium-high; order unconfirmed.

## 3. Josh Gudwin, Dua Lipa "Don't Start Now" (pop)
- Source: "Inside Track: Dua Lipa 'Don't Start Now'", Paul Tingen, SOS, May 2020. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-dua-lipa-dont-start-now
- Lead chain in order: Brainworx bx_cleansweep v2 (removes ~30 Hz sub so the compressor reacts to low mids); Goodhertz Vulf Compressor (light, grit); Waves LA-3A (smooth); Soothe (trim body); UAD Pultec EQP-1A (low cut a bit, +12 kHz air); FabFilter Pro-DS (highs boosted before it on purpose); Pro-Q2 (restore top lost in de-essing); Xfer LFO Tool (ducks vocal 10% per kick, up to 40% in verses). Producer Kirkpatrick prefers LFO Tool to sidechain for recovery control, told Gudwin not to compress the stem.
- Reverb: Valhalla VintageVerb then Xfer OTT, LFO copies vocal automation, then reverb compressed by UAD 1176.
- Vocal chop (bridge): Little AlterBoy (lower formant), MicroShift (width), Waves H-Delay dotted eighth very light placed before FabFilter Saturn (used as compressor via Dynamics, so the delay swells when the vocal stops, like sidechaining the delay), Waves C1 very fast attack/release, Pro-DS, LFOTool softening transient.
- Backing vocals: no detail given.
- Label: FACT. Confidence: high. Note a Gearspace-style summary elsewhere says "Gudwin"; article credit is Gudwin (mixer) with producer Ian Kirkpatrick.

## 4. Carlo "Illangelo" Montagnese, The Weeknd "The Hills" (pop/R&B)
- Source: "Inside Track: The Weeknd", Paul Tingen, SOS, Dec 2015. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-weeknd
- Verse lead inserts in order: Auto-Tune EFX (subtle), Pro-DS, Pro-Q2 (rolls lows/highs for lo-fi), Waves Vocal Rider, UAD API 560 (gentle, adds fuzz), Pro-MB. Parallel: Little Radiator then heavily squashed UAD 1176.
- Five sends from "All Lead Verse": Altiverb 7 with H-Delay pre-delay and Q2; UAD EP34 slap; Doubler then MicroShift then Q2 (highs reduced so doubled sibilance does not distract); Timeless 2 long delays filtered; second Altiverb.
- Chorus: same core plus Reel ADT, Decapitator (Punish, driven lightly), Volcano; flanger on a send.
- Ad-libs: Auto-Tune EFX, Q2, Vocal Rider, Pro-DS, Pro-G (gate); parallel Volcano track feeding EP34, Timeless 2, H-Delay+Altiverb.
- Backing vocals per track: Auto-Tune Live, Q2, Vocal Rider, Pro-G, Pro-DS; group: Pultec (cut lows), two mono LA-2As, Pultec (boost highs), API 560.
- Recorded raw, no EQ/comp before DAW; 42 mixes.
- Label: FACT. Confidence: high; no numeric settings.

## 5. Rob Kinelski, Billie Eilish "Bad Guy" (pop, dry/intimate)
- Source: "Inside Track: Billie Eilish 'Bad Guy'", Paul Tingen, SOS, Jul 2019. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-billie-eilish-bad-guy
- Four vocal auxes (Lead, Doubles, Harmonies, Backing), one shared chain with EQ varied per group; 21 vocal tracks.
- Lead chain in order: Pro-Q 2 (low cut only), Waves PuigChild 670 (light, no numbers), Waves De-Esser at 6557 Hz, second Pro-Q 2 (removes mids 200 Hz-2 kHz), UAD Neve 1073 EQ (colour), Waves Vocal Rider (~1.5 dB). Hand-drawn automation after. Optional Pro-DS; RVox would go before the 670 for another singer.
- Space: reverb was requested removed (album super dry); only "Width" aux with MicroShift. Delay none mentioned.
- Buses: Dangerous 2-Bus compressor 4:1 stereo-linked; Slate VMR on group buses; Oxford Inflator on backing bus.
- Label: FACT. Confidence: high. (A search summary also attributes Valhalla reverbs and Finneas effects to a different SOS page; not verified here.)

## 6. Taesub Lee, TWICE "Scientist" (K-pop)
- Source: "Inside Track: TWICE 'Scientist'", Paul Tingen, SOS, Feb 2022. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-twice-scientist?page=2
- Lead inserts: Waves CLA-76 (attack 3, release 5, vs his usual 4 and 6) then Pro-Q 3 (standard, high shelf adds top). CLA Mix Hub on a couple of tracks for radio-voice. Oxford Inflator on the lead folder.
- Four sends per lead: "Doubler" aux (SoundToys MicroShift), UAD Lexicon 480 reverb, TC 2290 delay, Waves H-Delay at eighth notes.
- Backing vocals (~70 tracks): CLA-76, Pro-Q 3, SSL Channel, Pro-DS, Devil-Loc; one send only, to the 480, so they sit in a different space; Waves S1 for width on one group.
- Parallel compression via UAD 1176AE on a rear bus (idea adapted from Andrew Scheps).
- Label: FACT. Confidence: high. Vocals arrive tuned/comped.

## 8. Serban Ghenea / John Hanes vocal chain (pop)
- Source: "Serban Ghenea Vocal Chain Revealed!", Matty Harris, mixandmastermysong.com, 20 Apr 2023. https://mixandmastermysong.com/serban-ghenea-vocal-chain-revealed/ (page spells "Haynes"; relays a Gearspace Q&A with Ghenea's partner; direct Gearspace link not given, so the underlying statement is UNVERIFIED at source).
- Chain as relayed: low cut (Avid Q3) if needed; Waves 1176 (Blackie ~3 dB at 4:1; Bluey ~10 dB at 4:1 for aggressive); Metric Halo Channel Strip EQ; Waves Renaissance De-Esser ~5500 Hz, ~6 dB, by ear; iZotope RX cleanup; Valhalla Vintage Verb; Waves H-Delay and Avid Mod Delay 3 with quarter/eighth settings. - Label: FACT by Hanes (second-hand via blog); not a named single record. Confidence: medium. Page has affiliate links. The page says "compression then EQ" but also lists low-cut first.

## 11. Jaycen Joshua, Justin Bieber "Baby" (pop with rap feature)
- Source: "Secrets Of The Mix Engineers: Jaycen Joshua", Paul Tingen, SOS, Aug 2010. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-jaycen-joshua

## 12. Tom Elmhirst, Adele "Rolling In The Deep" (pop/soul)
- Source: "Tom Elmhirst: Recording Adele 'Rolling In The Deep'", Paul Tingen, SOS, Sep 2011. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/tom-elmhirst-recording-adele-rolling-deep
- Plug-ins: Waves Q6 notches 930 Hz (-18 dB), 1634 Hz (-18 dB), 3175 Hz (-12.1 dB) on chorus vocal; two Waves De-essers at 5449 Hz and 11004 Hz.
- Console insert: Pultec (boost ~100 Hz and 12k, little gain), then blackface 1176 (fast attack/release), then Tube-Tech CL1B (slow attack/release, evening level).
- Sends: ridden on faders, up to four vocal sends (reverb, chorus, delay...). Epworth's RE-501 Space Echo track was the only lead effect in first chorus; spring reverbs on backing vocals; delays on choruses for depth.
- Quotes: "on the vocal you mainly want control"; "If you're constantly moving the fader... you know that you need to look at your compression."
- Label: FACT. Confidence: high.
- Related (not opened, search snippets only): Elmhirst on "Hello" used no compression, only de-essing and small rides (SonicScoop, https://sonicscoop.com/inside-adeles-25-how-tom-elmhirst-mixed-a-masterpiece/); Winehouse "Rehab" Pultec into 1176 into Fairchild (SOS https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-tom-elmhirst). UNVERIFIED (not opened).

## 13. iZotope, "Crafting a basic vocal chain"
- Source: Nick Messitte, iZotope, 21 Sep 2023. https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/crafting-a-basic-vocal-chain
- Chain: pitch correction first (works best on dry signal); EQ before compressor (acknowledged as debated); compressor ~3-7 dB GR on lead (more risks squashed); de-esser (tame sibilance above 3 kHz; sibilance often rises after high-end EQ boost); effects last (delay then reverb order changes the sound; roll off lows/highs on delays and reverb; L/R delay offset 1/64 with feedback zero for widening).
- No ratio, attack, release or EQ frequencies given.
- Label: CONVENTION (manufacturer, workflow). Confidence: high for stated points; promotes Nectar.

## 14. iZotope, de-essing dos and don'ts
- Source: Nick Messitte, iZotope, 7 May 2025. https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/the-dos-and-donts-of-de-essing
- Common advice: de-esser after EQ before compressor, but last, first, or two (after EQ and after compressor) are all valid. De-essing before EQ leaves room for top-end boost. Clip gain/automation first. Consider de-essing sends before reverb or distortion. Sibilance typically 4-10 kHz, as low as 1.5 kHz, singer-dependent; ~3 kHz when belting.
- Label: CONVENTION. Confidence: high.
- Conflict with iZotope's own basic-chain article only in emphasis (de-ess after compression there). Andrew Scheps de-esses at the start of the vocal chain and often again at the end (Gearspace Q&A, search snippet only, UNVERIFIED).

## 15. Greg Ogan (Recording Magazine), LA-style vocal sessions
- Source: "Vocal Sessions, LA Style", Greg Ogan, Recording Magazine, undated (c) 2022 notice. https://www.recordingmag.com/resources/recording-info/mics-miking/vocal-sessions-la-style/
- Tracking: rock U67/SM7 + Neve + 1176/Distressor; R&B/pop Sony C800 + Millennia STT-1; cut lows near 100 Hz, small boost 14-16 kHz; 3-6 dB GR, moderately fast attack/release.
- Stacking: organic = lead + two doubles panned ~50% L/R; pop = lead 1/3/5 parts, harmonies 2 or 4 per note; 5-part lead pans centre, 100% and 50% L/R; R&B up to 8 tracks per part. Each part has its own bus; lead compressed before a bus already compressed (serial).
- Label: FACT (his workflow). Confidence: medium-high.

## 16. Vocal throw delay
- Source: "The Vocal Throw", Ivan Meshcheriakov, Meshplugins (plugin vendor blog), dated 25 Sep 2026 in the page. https://shop.meshplugins.com/blogs/the-hidden-sauce/vocal-throw-delay
- Settings: quarter (500 ms at 120 BPM) or dotted 1/8 (375 ms); feedback 30-45% (~35% gives 4-5 repeats, >60% bleeds); return 100% wet, HP ~400 Hz, LP 4-5 kHz; automate the send (-inf, then a 0 dB rectangle across the word), not the mix knob; open-vowel word endings; duck instrumental bus 1 dB for ~half a bar; 2-3 throws per song; check ping-pong in mono.
- Label: CONVENTION (vendor blog, no named record). Confidence: medium.

## Conflicts between sources
- EQ vs compressor order: iZotope puts EQ before compressor but calls it debated; Ghenea/Hanes (as relayed) say compression first then EQ; Pensado puts only EQ first, no compression; Elmhirst puts Pultec before 1176 before CL1B.
- De-esser position: after EQ before compressor (iZotope "common advice"), after compression (iZotope basic chain, Hanes, Eilish), first or last or two (iZotope), Scheps at start plus end (UNVERIFIED), Gudwin/Dua Lipa after EQ near end of chain.
- Compression amount: iZotope 3-7 dB; Ghenea ~3 or ~10 dB; Ogan 3-6 dB; Elmhirst "control", and no compression on "Hello"; Pensado dislikes compression.
- Reverb on rap: Joshua says reverb is "the kiss of death on rap vocals" (but used delay/ping-pong); Drake "Headlines" uses Reverb aux (no settings); Ali uses reverb aux in the last section.
- Reverb pre-delay: Pensado 16th or 32nd note; blogs 20-60 ms; Harris 16th triplet. Not in conflict if converted by tempo, but no source states ms for a named record.
