## 2. Melodic Architecture and Flow Cadence

### 2a. Scale Choices, Darkness, and Harmonic Moods (CONVENTION / FACT)
- **Natural Minor (Aeolian) & Dorian**: Foundation of boom-bap, jazz-hop, and conscious hip hop (J Dilla, A Tribe Called Quest, Kendrick Lamar, J. Cole). Relies on sampled minor 7th, 9th, and 11th chord extensions.
- **Phrygian & Phrygian Dominant (1, b2, b3, 4, 5, b6, b7)**: The signature harmonic identity of trap (Metro Boomin, Southside, 21 Savage). The minor 2nd (semitone above tonic) provides instantaneous menace, tension, and danger.
- **Harmonic Minor (1, 2, b3, 4, 5, b6, 7)**: Key to UK and NY Drill (Pop Smoke, Central Cee). The augmented second between b6 and 7 gives piano and bell motifs an ominous, cinematic gothic feel.
- **Minor Pentatonic Vocal Hooks**: In melodic rap (Juice WRLD, Post Malone, Lil Baby), top-line hooks stay strictly within 3 to 5 notes of the minor pentatonic scale, optimizing earworm retention and sing-along appeal.

### 2b. Flow, Cadence as Melody, and Vocal Delivery
- **Rhythmic Cadence as Melody**: In rap, flow *is* melody. Vocal delivery functions as a lead pitched percussion instrument that interlocks rhythmically with the kick, snare, and hi-hats.
- **The Triplet / "Migos Flow" (FACT)**: 8th-note triplet metric division over 4/4 meter. Popularized by Three 6 Mafia and mainstreamed by Migos ("Versace", "Bad and Boujee"), Kendrick Lamar, and Drake.
- **Hard Auto-Tune as an Instrument (CONVENTION)**: Modern melodic trap utilizes Antares Auto-Tune set to 0 ms retune speed to force vocal pitch transitions into discrete, quantized synthesizer-like glides (Travis Scott, Future).

## 3. Rhythmic Architectures, Grooves, and Tempos

### 3a. Tempo Archetypes and the Half-Time Grid
- **Boom-Bap / Golden Age**: 84 - 96 BPM. Straight 4/4 meter. Kick on 1 and syncopated variations; snappy acoustic snare or layered rim-clap strictly on beats 2 and 4.
- **Modern Trap**: 130 - 160 BPM in the DAW, perceived at **half-time (65 - 80 BPM)**. The primary snare or clap hits strictly on **Beat 3** (the half-time backbeat), giving immense open space for rapid hi-hat rolls.
- **UK / NY Drill**: 140 - 146 BPM. Syncopated bounce where the snare hits on Beat 3 in bar 1, followed by an unexpected hit on the "and" of beat 4 in bar 2, creating a lurching sway.
- **West Coast G-Funk / Bay Area Hyphy**: 94 - 104 BPM. Heavy 16th-note swing, rolling basslines, and handclaps on 2 and 4.

### 3b. Swing, Quantization, and Micro-Timing
- **MPC 16th-Note Swing (54% - 62%) (FACT)**: Roger Linn's swing algorithms on the MPC60 and MPC3000, delaying alternate 16th notes to produce the head-nod bounce of 90s hip hop.
- **J Dilla Unquantized Timing (FACT)**: Disabling grid quantize entirely; laying kick drums slightly early (rushing) and snares slightly late (dragging) to generate tension and polyrhythmic human groove.
- **Trap Hi-Hat Rolls and Pitch Automation**: Rapid switches between 1/16th, 1/32nd, 1/32nd triplets (1/48), and 1/64th subdivisions, paired with downward pitch automations and stereo panning.

## 4. Songwriting, Form, and Circular Harmony

### 4a. The Circular Loop (CONVENTION)
- Unlike classical or pop functional harmony that resolves (V -> I), hip hop relies on non-resolving 2-bar or 4-bar circular loops. The progression repeats endlessly without tension resolution, allowing the rapper's cadence and arrangement mute automation to define the journey.
- **Common Progressions**:
  - Sad / Emo Trap: i - VI - III - VII (e.g. C#m - A - E - B; Juice WRLD, Post Malone).
  - Menacing Trap: i - bII (Phrygian vamp: Am - Bb or static root drone; Metro Boomin).
  - Soulful Boom-Bap: i9 - iv7 - bVII9 - IIImaj7 (jazz turnaround loop).

### 4b. Sample Manipulation Techniques (FACT)
- **Resampling and Pitch-Shifting**: Recording an original melody on piano or guitar, rendering to audio, pitching down 2 to 5 semitones, slowing down 50% (half-time), and applying tape wow/flutter (Frank Dukes, Cubeatz).

## 5. Production, Sound Design, and Mixing Strategies

### 5a. The 808 Bass Architecture (FACT / CONVENTION)
- **Sine Wave Fundamental + Harmonic Saturation**: Pure sub-bass frequencies (30 - 60 Hz) carry the weight, while tube or tape saturation generates odd and even harmonics (120 - 300 Hz) so the 808 translates on mobile phone speakers.
- **Kick vs. 808 Frequency Allocation**:
  - *Option A (Modern Trap Standard)*: Short, punchy kick with fundamental at 60-90 Hz; long, booming 808 carrying sub-lows at 35-55 Hz.
  - *Option B (Boom-Bap / Drill)*: Low, thumping kick at 45-55 Hz; higher pitched, distorted bass or 808 sitting at 70-110 Hz.
- **Sidechaining or Volume Carving**: Truncating the first 20-40 ms of the 808 note or applying fast sidechain ducking so the kick's transient punches through cleanly without phase collision.

### 5b. Vocal Staging and Space
- **Lead Rap Vocal**: 100% dry and upfront in the center; compressed aggressively with fast FET (1176) into optical leveling (LA-2A).
- **Ad-Libs and Doubled Words**: Panned hard left and right (80-100% L/R), filtered with telephone bandpass EQ (high-pass at 500 Hz, low-pass at 4 kHz), with heavy stereo ping-pong delay and wide reverb.

## 6. Implementation and Beat Arrangement Matrix

| Section | Length | Drum Elements | Bass / 808 | Melodic Elements | Vocal Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Intro** | 4-8 bars | Filtered hats / shaker | Muted | Filtered sample / keys | Spoken intro / tag |
| **Hook 1** | 8 bars | Full kit: Kick, clap, 808 | Full 808 groove | Full melody loop + counter | Melodic chorus / catchy hook |
| **Verse 1A**| 8 bars | Kick + clap (no 808) | Tacit / low drone | Core sample loop only | Rap verse delivery |
| **Verse 1B**| 8 bars | Full drums enter | 808 re-enters | Counter-melody / flute enters| Rising vocal urgency |
| **Pre-Hook** | 4 bars | Hats only / kick drops out| Muted | Reverb swell / riser | Melodic transition line |
| **Hook 2** | 8 bars | Full kit + open hats | Full 808 slides | All melodies + lead synth | Maximum hook impact |
| **Verse 2** | 12-16 bars| Syncopated variation | Sub-bass switch | Stripped to piano/chords | Complex flow patterns |
| **Bridge** | 4-8 bars | Half-time kick / muffled | Filtered sub | Reverse sweeps / chops | Introspective vocal breakdown|
| **Final Hook**| 8-16 bars| Maximum percussion | Aggressive 808 | Full layers + ad-lib tracks | Hook + ad-lib layered |
| **Outro** | 4-8 bars | Kick drops, hats fade | Sub fade | Filter sweep to darkness | Producer tag / fade out |
