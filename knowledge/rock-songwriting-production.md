## 1. Scope and how to read this page

- Covers melodic construction, rhythmic groove/pocket, song architecture, harmonic progressions, and multi-track production strategies across classic rock, alternative rock, grunge, punk, and hard rock.

## 2. Melodic Architecture and Motif Design

### 2a. Scale Choices and Modal Colors (CONVENTION / FACT)
- **Minor Pentatonic and Blues Scale (1, b3, 4, b5, 5, b7)**: The harmonic spine of hard rock and classic rock (Led Zeppelin, AC/DC, Black Sabbath). The diminished 5th (b5 "blue note") adds friction, bite, and chromatic grit when played against major third or power chord backings.
- **Mixolydian Mode (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, b7)**: The definitive anthem rock scale (The Rolling Stones, Guns N' Roses, Tom Petty). The flat-7th avoids the polite classical leading tone while retaining a driving, triumphant major 3rd.
- **Dorian Mode (1, 2, b3, 4, 5, 6, b7)**: Blends minor attitude with major lift (Pink Floyd, Soundgarden, Santana). The natural 6th injects blues-rock swagger and prevents gloom.
- **Aeolian Mode (Natural Minor: 1, 2, b3, 4, 5, b6, b7)**: The foundation of 90s grunge and modern alternative rock (Nirvana, Alice in Chains, Linkin Park), delivering brooding emotional gravity.

### 2b. The Riff-as-Hook and Phrasing Archetypes
- **The Riff-as-Hook (CONVENTION)**: In rock, the electric guitar riff functions as the primary melodic hook alongside or exceeding the vocal melody (e.g. Deep Purple "Smoke on the Water", Nirvana "Smells Like Teen Spirit", The White Stripes "Seven Nation Army").
- **Call-and-Response Phrasing**: Alternating vocal lines with instrumental fills (guitar licks or drum fills). Leaving 2-4 beats of vocal silence for instrumental commentary keeps high energy without overcrowding the frequency spectrum.
- **Tessitura and Vocal Intensity Leaps (FACT)**: Verses sit in low/conversational chest voice; choruses leap an octave or major 6th into full-throttle mixed belt or raspy scream to trigger emotional catharsis.

## 3. Rhythmic Architecture, Groove, and Tempos

### 3a. Tempo Archetypes and Subdivision
- **Mid-Tempo Rock Groove**: 115 - 130 BPM (AC/DC "Back in Black" @ 92 half-time / 184; Foo Fighters "Everlong" @ 158; Led Zeppelin "Rock and Roll" @ 170).
- **Grunge / Sludge / Heavy Alternative**: 75 - 95 BPM (Soundgarden "Black Hole Sun" @ 52; Nirvana "Come As You Are" @ 120; Alice in Chains "Rooster" @ 72).
- **Punk / Pop-Punk**: 155 - 195 BPM (Green Day "Basket Case" @ 175; Blink-182 "All The Small Things" @ 148).
- **Power Ballad**: 65 - 80 BPM (Pink Floyd "Comfortably Numb" @ 64; Aerosmith "Dream On" @ 80).

### 3b. Pocket and Human Timing Mechanics
- **The Bonham "Behind-the-Beat" Snare Drag (FACT)**: Kick drum is planted firmly on the downbeat, but the snare is laid micro-seconds *behind* the beat, generating massive perceived size and heavy pocket.
- **The Punk "Ahead-of-the-Beat" Snare Rush (CONVENTION)**: Snare and upbeats lean micro-seconds *ahead* of the click, creating urgency, adrenaline, and forward momentum.
- **Straight 8th Driving Pulse**: Guitars and bass lock into continuous downstroke 8th-note pulses with accents on beats 1 and 3, anchoring the rhythm section.

## 4. Songwriting, Form, and Harmonic Progressions

### 4a. Common Rock Chord Progressions
- **The Flat-VII Rock Turnaround (CONVENTION)**: I - bVII - IV (e.g. D - C - G; AC/DC, Lynyrd Skynyrd, Guns N' Roses). The subtonic bVII chord provides modal power without standard V-I classical resolution.
- **The Grunge Aeolian Cadence**: i - VI - III - VII (e.g. Em - C - G - D) or i - bVI - bVII (Nirvana, Pearl Jam).
- **Modal Mixture / Borrowed Chords (FACT)**: Borrowing the bVI or bIII from the parallel minor in a major key (e.g. C - Eb - F - C) gives classic rock its heavy, triumphant weight (The Who, The Beatles).
- **Power Chords (Root-5th Dyads)**: Omitting the 3rd eliminates minor/major harmonic conflict, enabling heavy distortion without dissonant intermodulation mud.

### 4b. Dynamic Macro-Structure: The Loud-Quiet-Loud Dynamic (FACT)
- **Verse**: Minimalist, sparse, clean or chorused guitars, dry intimate bass, closed hi-hats.
- **Pre-Chorus**: Rising tom rolls, introducing overdrive, expanding rhythm parts.
- **Chorus (Wall of Sound)**: Quad-tracked distorted guitars, open crash cymbals, bass moving to aggressive pick drive, belting double-tracked vocals.
- **Bridge**: Metric modulation, tempo half-time, or shift to unexpected minor key, clearing the palate before the final climax chorus.

## 5. Production, Sound Design, and Arrangement Techniques

### 5a. Guitar Orchestration and Layering (CONVENTION / FACT)
- **Quad-Tracking Rhythm Guitars**: Recording two takes on one amp/guitar combination panned 100% L/R, and two takes on an alternate amp/guitar voiced brighter or with higher midrange panned 80% L/R.
- **Amp Pairing Principle**: Pair a mid-focused amplifier (Marshall JCM800) with a scooped, low-end heavy amplifier (Mesa Dual Rectifier or Peavey 5150) for a composite guitar wall that fills the entire frequency spectrum.
- **Clean Underpinning (The "Shadow" Guitar)**: Tucking an ultra-clean, DI, or acoustic guitar strum underneath heavily distorted walls adds crisp transient attack without altering perceived distortion.

### 5b. Room Capture and Drum Production
- **Ambient Room Mics (The Glyn Johns / Andy Wallace Method)**: Blending crushed stereo room mics (compressing room mics by 10-15 dB with fast limiters) to provide the acoustic glue and explosive decay of the kit.
- **Bass DI + Amp Bi-Amping**: Routing the low end (< 150 Hz) cleanly via DI for solid low-end pitch, while passing the mid and high frequencies (> 250 Hz) through a dirty SVT or guitar distortion pedal for attack and bite.

## 6. Implementation and Arrangement Matrix

| Section | Instrumentation Density | Dynamic Level | Drum Pattern | Vocal Staging |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Intro** | Isolated riff or bass hook | Medium | Ghost snare / hi-hat only | Tacit |
| **Verse 1** | Clean/arpeggiated guitar + bass | Low | Kick + closed hat + rimshot | Single dry lead vocal |
| **Pre-Chorus** | Building rhythm guitar + overdrive | Med-High | Floor tom build / crescendo | Vocal harmonies enter |
| **Chorus 1** | Quad distorted guitars + wide bass | High | Full backbeat + ride/crash | Doubled vocal + octave |
| **Verse 2** | Verse 1 + subtle lead guitar fill | Low-Med | Kick + snare on 2 & 4 | Single lead vocal |
| **Chorus 2** | Full wall of sound | Maximum | Driving crashes + kick | Full vocal stack |
| **Bridge** | Half-time feel or riff breakdown | Low to Peak | Half-time snare on beat 3 | Raw emotional delivery |
| **Solo** | Lead guitar + driving rhythm section | High | Relentless driving 8ths | Tacit |
| **Final Chorus**| Maximum layers + ad-lib vocals | Peak | Accented snare and double kicks| Belting + screaming |
| **Outro** | Repeating riff or feedback fade | High -> Low | Free-time cymbal swells / crash | Ad-lib repeats |
