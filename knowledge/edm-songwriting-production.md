## 2. Melodic Architecture and Synth Hooks

### 2a. Scale Choices, Modal Colors, and Festival Hooks (CONVENTION / FACT)
- **Aeolian (Natural Minor) & Minor Pentatonic**: The primary harmonic palette for progressive house, trance, and melodic techno (Avicii "Levels", Swedish House Mafia "Don't You Worry Child", Deadmau5 "Strobe"). Balances emotional nostalgia and grandeur with energetic physical release at the drop.
- **Dorian Mode**: The core harmonic identity of French house, disco house, and UK garage (Daft Punk, Disclosure). The bright natural 6th injects funk and groove over steady house beats.
- **Phrygian Mode (1, b2, b3, 4, 5, b6, b7)**: Dominates dark techno, psytrance, and heavy dubstep/riddim (Skrillex, Excision). The minor second semitone drone over the root creates menacing, aggressive tension.
- **Vocal Chops as Lead Instruments (FACT)**: Slicing, pitching, and formant-shifting 1-2 bar vocal phrases into rhythmic, monophonic synth-like lead melodies (Skrillex, DJ Snake, Martin Garrix).

### 2b. Arpeggiated Ostinatos and Portamento
- **16th-Note Arpeggiated Ostinatos (CONVENTION)**: Lead synth lines often consist of rapid 16th-note arpeggiated motifs that repeat continuously over shifting bassline chords, acting as hypnotic melodic anchors (Deadmau5, Eric Prydz).
- **Pitch Glides & Portamento**: Monophonic lead synths and sub-bass notes set to 30 - 120 ms glide times, sliding between octaves or from the flat-7th to the root to generate pitch excitement.
- **Call-and-Response Drop Phrasing**: 2-bar or 4-bar drop units: Bar 1-2 presents a heavy growl/bass riff ("Call"), followed by Bar 3-4 answering with a percussive stab or vocal catchphrase ("Response").

## 3. Rhythmic Architectures, Grooves, and Tempos

### 3a. Tempo Archetypes and Sub-Genres
- **House / Tech-House**: 124 - 128 BPM. Straight 4/4 meter. Kick on every quarter note (1, 2, 3, 4); open hi-hat on every upbeat ("and"); clap/snare on beats 2 and 4.
- **Dubstep / Future Bass**: 140 - 150 BPM (perceived at **half-time: 70 - 75 BPM**). Kick on Beat 1; massive snare on **Beat 3** (half-time backbeat).
- **Drum & Bass (D&B)**: 172 - 176 BPM. High-speed syncopated two-step breakbeat: Kick on 1, snare on 2, syncopated kick on the "and" of 3, snare on 4.
- **Trance / Psytrance**: 138 - 142 BPM. Driving four-on-the-floor kick with a rolling triplet 16th "gallop" bassline (kick - bass - bass - bass).

### 3b. Quantization, Swing, and Micro-Timing
- **100% Rigid Grid Quantization**: Four-on-the-floor kicks and sub-basses in festival EDM are locked strictly to the grid to preserve phase alignment with sidechain envelopes and sub-bass oscillators.
- **Groove Swing in House / UK Garage (FACT)**: Applying 54% - 58% MPC 16th-note swing to hats, percussive shakers, and ghost snares to inject human bounce into the rigid kick foundation.
- **Pre-Shifted Transients (Ahead-of-the-Beat Rush)**: Claps and snares are nudged 5 to 15 ms early (ahead of the grid) or layered with pre-shifted noise sweeps to lead into beats 2 and 4 with explosive attack.

## 4. Songwriting, Macro-Form, and Energy Dynamics

### 4a. The Drop Economy (Macro-Structure) (CONVENTION)
- **DJ Intro (16 - 32 bars)**: Stripped-down kick, percussion, and basic bass loop designed for DJ beatmatching.
- **Verse / Breakdown (8 - 16 bars)**: Kick drum is completely removed. Vocals, pads, and emotional chord progressions take center stage.
- **Build-up / Riser (8 - 16 bars)**: High-pass filters sweep out all sub frequencies; snare rolls accelerate (1/4 -> 1/8 -> 1/16 -> 1/32); pitch risers and white noise sweeps escalate tension to maximum.
- **Pre-Drop Silence (The Drop Fill, 1 to 2 beats) (FACT)**: Absolute momentary silence or an isolated vocal catchphrase immediately before beat 1 of the drop. Cutting energy to zero makes the ensuing drop feel devastatingly massive.
- **The Drop (16 - 32 bars)**: Peak energy release. Full kick, sub-bass, and lead synth hook arrive at full loudness. Divided into Drop A (first 16 bars) and Drop B (additional percussion/counter-melody for last 16 bars).
- **Breakdown 2 -> Build-up 2 -> Drop 2 (Peak)**: Second energy cycle with intensified sound design or melodic variations.
- **DJ Outro (16 - 32 bars)**: Elements strip away progressively for outgoing DJ mixing.

### 4b. Harmonic Loops in Dance Music
- **The Emotional 4-Chord Minor Loop**:
  - vi - IV - I - V (e.g. Am - F - C - G; Swedish House Mafia, Avicii).
  - i - bVII - bVI - bVII (e.g. Cm - Bb - Ab - Bb; classic trance and progressive house).
- **The Static Single-Root Drone (Tech-House / Techno)**: No chord progressions; the entire track stays anchored to a single minor root note (e.g. F minor), generating interest through filter cutoff automation and rhythmic layering.

## 5. Production, Sound Design, and Mixing Strategies

### 5a. Sidechain Pumping and Volume Envelope Ducking (FACT)
- **Kick-to-Bass Sidechain Ducking**: The kick and sub-bass never play at full volume simultaneously. When the kick fires, the sub-bass and synths are ducked by 12 dB to complete silence for 50-150 ms via volume automation envelopes (Cableguys VolumeShaper, Kickstart, or fast sidechain compression).
- **Pumping Pad / Lead Swell**: Ducking pads and supersaw synths on every quarter note and swelling back up on the offbeats ("four-on-the-floor pump").

### 5b. Frequency Allocation and Spatial Contrast
- **Mono Low-End Rule (CONVENTION)**: All audio below 100 - 120 Hz (sub-bass, kick fundamental) is strictly summed to mono to prevent destructive phase cancellation on club sound systems.
- **Stereo Collapse and Explode (FACT)**: During the build-up, stereo width is narrowed using utility width plugins until the pre-drop fill is near-mono. When the drop arrives, wide unison supersaws (panned 100% L/R) and stereo reverbs explode outward, creating a massive psychoacoustic impact.
- **Multiband Dynamics (The OTT Phenomenon) (FACT)**: Upward and downward multiband compression (Xfer OTT) lifts low-level harmonics while clamping peaks, producing hyper-aggressive, dense synth textures.
